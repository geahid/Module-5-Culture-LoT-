Lesson 1 Internet of Things (IoT)

What is IoT?

Definition of IoTDefinition▼

The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to the network of physical devices embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity that enables them to collect, send, and act on data from their environments. These devices — from lightbulbs and refrigerators to smart watches and security cameras — turn physical information into digital data.

By 2020, the number of internet-connected devices exceeded 20 billion worldwide.

Who Coined the Term "Internet of Things"?History▼

The term was coined by Kevin Ashton in 1999 to describe the concept of giving computers "eyes and ears" through connected objects.

The first IoT device was a toaster created by John Romkey in 1990, which he controlled with his computer.

How IoT Works

IoT Ecosystem & ProcessHow It Works▼
1
Collect — Smart devices use embedded processors, sensors, and communication hardware to collect data from their environment.
2
Send — Data is sent to an IoT gateway or edge device via network connectivity protocols.
3
Analyze — Data is either analyzed locally or sent to the cloud for processing. AI and machine learning may be used.
4
Act — Devices act on the information, often without human intervention, though humans can still interact with them.

Why IoT Matters

Importance of IoTImportance▼

IoT helps people live and work smarter by automating homes and giving businesses real-time insight into their systems — from machine performance to supply chain operations.

For businesses, IoT can:

  • Monitor overall business processes
  • Improve the customer experience
  • Save time and money; enhance employee productivity
  • Automate tasks and reduce labor costs
  • Generate more revenue and enable better decisions
  • Integrate and adapt business models

IoT is most common in manufacturing, transportation, and utilities, but also appears in agriculture, infrastructure, and home automation.

Pros & Cons of IoT

Advantages and Disadvantages of IoTPros & Cons▼

✓ Advantages

  • Access information anywhere, anytime, on any device
  • Improved communication between connected devices
  • Transfers data packets quickly, saving time and money
  • Automates tasks, reducing need for human intervention

✗ Disadvantages

  • More devices = greater risk of hackers stealing confidential info
  • Managing millions of devices is complex and challenging
  • A bug in the system can corrupt every connected device
  • No international compatibility standard — devices from different manufacturers may not communicate
Lesson 2 Current Trends & Emerging Technologies

Current Trends in IT

a. Cloud ComputingCurrent Trend▼

Cloud computing is a network of digital resources that companies can access instead of relying on local storage. It increases organizational efficiency and reduces infrastructure costs.

  • 83% of enterprise workloads were projected to be in the cloud by 2020 (Forbes)
  • Enables cloud storage, employee collaboration, and virtualization
  • Less installation needed on individual computers — the Internet provides direct access
  • Organizations can offer cloud services as external products to generate revenue
b. Mobile Computing and ApplicationsCurrent Trend▼

Mobile phones, tablets, and apps have transformed both business and personal life. 77% of Americans own smartphones — a 35% increase since 2011 (Pew Research Center).

  • Mobile traffic and app use continue to skyrocket
  • More users access the internet via phones than traditional broadband
  • Fastest-growing companies have mobile websites, marketing, and apps
  • Cloud apps enable on-the-go capabilities for businesses
c. Big Data AnalyticsCurrent Trend▼

Big data enables businesses to analyze massive sets of information with high volume, variety, and velocity. It offers a high return on investment by powering data-driven decisions.

  • Data mining helps companies predict growth and achieve future success
  • Boosts productivity of marketing campaigns
  • Database maintenance is a growing IT career sector
  • Converts leads into paying customers through insight-driven strategy
d. AutomationCurrent Trend▼

Automation uses machine processes to replace repetitive manual tasks, increasing productivity and freeing employees to focus on higher-value work.

  • Can collect and process information from vendors, customers, and documentation
  • Automates invoicing, accounts-payable, and customer interactions
  • Creates new IT job opportunities in programming and supporting automated systems
  • Machine learning enhances automation for continuously improving systems
  • Future applications include automated grocery payments and consumer streamlining

Emerging Trends in IT

a. Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Smart MachinesEmerging▼

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the ability of a digital computer or robot to perform tasks associated with intelligent beings — reasoning, learning, pattern recognition, and decision-making — faster and more accurately than humans.

Current AI Applications:

  • Face Recognition — helps find missing persons and identify criminal suspects from camera footage
  • Medical AI — predicts treatment outcomes, estimates hospital stay length, and analyzes health records using deep learning
  • Home AI — voice assistants (Alexa, Google Assistant, Siri, Bixby) handle daily tasks like music, directions, and messaging
b. Virtual Reality (VR)Emerging▼

Virtual Reality (VR) uses computer modeling and simulation to let users interact with an artificial 3D visual or sensory environment. Users are fully immersed via wearable devices.

  • Devices include goggles, headsets, gloves, and body suits
  • Motion sensors adjust the view in real time based on the user's movements (telepresence)
  • Data gloves with force-feedback allow users to "touch" and manipulate virtual objects
  • The Head-Mounted Display (HMD) is VR's most recognizable component
  • Display technology is the key difference between immersive VR and traditional interfaces
c. Augmented Reality (AR)Emerging▼

Augmented Reality (AR) is a more versatile and practical version of VR — it does not fully immerse the user. Instead, it overlays digital images, sounds, and information on top of the real world.

  • Features interactive scenarios that enhance (not replace) real-world experience
  • Most well-known example: Pokémon Go
  • Applications in retail, education, navigation, and industrial training

d. Blockchain DataEmerging▼

Blockchain is a system of recording information in a way that makes it nearly impossible to change, hack, or cheat. It is essentially a digital ledger of transactions duplicated and distributed across an entire network.

  • Data can be added but not changed, replaced, or deleted — ensuring integrity
  • Authority is shared across many parties, reducing over-reliance on specific staff
  • Associated with cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin
  • Especially useful for medical records, financial transactions, and IT security
  • Protects private data — both medical and IT industries benefit greatly
e. Internet of Things (IoT) as an Emerging TrendEmerging▼

Beyond smart homes, IoT is an emerging business force. Cars, homes, appliances, and other products now connect to the Internet — acting like a digital personal assistant for users and organizations alike.

  • Voice recognition allows access to cloud-stored data hands-free
  • Helps organizations with customer interactions, remote data collection, and confirmations
  • Enables smart features: locking doors remotely, hands-free music, auto payments
f. 5G NetworkEmerging▼

5G is the 5th generation mobile network — a new global wireless standard that connects virtually everyone and everything: machines, objects, and devices.

  • Delivers higher multi-Gbps speeds, ultra-low latency, and massive network capacity
  • More reliable, more available, and more uniform user experience than previous generations
  • A unified air interface designed for next-generation user experiences
  • Will impact every industry: safer transportation, remote healthcare, precision agriculture, digitized logistics
